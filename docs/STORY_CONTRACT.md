# Story contract — Moriondo Cycle I

**Status:** Active and authoritative  
**Supersedes:** place-bound Moriondo, evil-Tom mirror, self-imprisonment and song-empty-stall prototypes  
**Scope:** `glasgefaengnis` media, review UI and all future JGA Fellowship OS integration

This document is the single active canon for the arc. Historical notes and prototypes may describe earlier concepts, but they must be labelled as superseded and must not drive active UI, dialogue, animation prompts or JGA state.

---

## 1. The antagonist

Moriondo is the principal active antagonist of this arc.

He is:

- physically embodied;
- genuinely defeated at Dol Guldur;
- physically confined inside the circular magical glass prison;
- highly intelligent, psychopathic, narcissistic and manipulative;
- capable of controlled performance and genuine uncontrolled rage;
- still dangerous because he can turn information, trust and collective judgement into instruments.

His capture is not voluntary. He did not build the entire defeat as a secret victory. The contingency he activates after capture does not invalidate the defeat.

The Blue Wizards are separate figures with a separate arc. Sauron is historical background, not the active organising antagonist here.

---

## 2. The first consequence: the empty stalls

After Moriondo is imprisoned, a contingency command already planted in the Düsterwald web activates.

Lower spiders physically abduct the mounts alive. In the stalls they leave:

- empty spaces;
- abandoned tack;
- thick cobwebs;
- drag and tension marks;
- individual knot structures;
- a trace leading into the Düsterwald.

The abduction is real. It is not a song, metaphor, projection or rewritten bond.

The mounts remain recoverable. The owner has explicitly approved visible controlled injury or blood where the silk bites in the abduction flashback. This does not permit death, dismemberment, torture spectacle or permanent loss.

---

## 3. Steven’s spider

Steven’s spider receives the same command as the lower brood, but its will is stronger.

It:

- resists Moriondo;
- forces the nearest lower spiders beneath its own authority;
- remains behind;
- preserves the command echo;
- can later transmit a completed counter-command.

It is indispensable but insufficient.

Steven’s spider cannot determine the correct brood, path, nest or severance instruction on its own. The Fellowship must discover and corroborate those elements collectively before the spider can act.

The proof that Steven’s spider broke Moriondo’s command is the principal narcissistic wound that triggers Moriondo’s genuine rage break.

---

## 4. The mount investigation

The visible crisis is the rescue of the mounts.

The cooperative logic is built around:

- private observations;
- deliberate release to the council;
- independent corroboration;
- contradiction and refutation;
- role-dependent unlocks;
- a final evidence-backed counter-command.

`Freigeben` means sharing an observation. It does not mean accepting it as true. Moriondo repeatedly tries to collapse disclosure, belief and consent into one another; the game interface must keep them separate.

The final counter-command requires four proven elements:

1. **Brut** — which lower brood carried or maintains the relevant route;
2. **Pfad** — the genuine path through the Düsterwald;
3. **Hort** — the actual holding site;
4. **Schnitt** — the point at which the returning command must be severed from Moriondo.

No single rider may possess the complete answer.

---

## 5. The hidden centre: Jan

The mount nests are not the deepest purpose of the web.

A further structure is discovered that belongs to none of the mounts:

- older than the lower brood’s ordinary silk;
- architecturally larger;
- built to bind more than an animal body;
- initially unreadable and deliberately non-identifying.

The late revelation is Jan, held inside the great cocoon.

The surprise must feel inevitable in retrospect:

> The strongest nest was never made for a mount.

The first two cocoon assets must not reveal Jan’s face or allow a reliable early identification. A readable Jan reveal requires his canonical JGA identity and explicit owner approval.

Jan’s release begins his app arc. It does not complete his story.

---

## 6. The ancient silk

The great cocoon is bound with **Urseide der Großen Verzehrerin**: an original Fellowship-OS concept descended from an ancient devouring brood and rebound through Moriondo’s newer command craft.

It is not a literal depiction of Shelob or Ungoliant, and it must not copy an existing film creature.

The lower spiders can maintain the outer network. They cannot create or destroy the innermost binding. Steven’s counter-command can expose and loosen the structure, but cannot alone break the ancient silk.

---

## 7. The Phial

The great cocoon requires a counter-artifact.

The new Phial joins:

- inherited light from the old Phial tradition;
- surrendered and transformed rider-runes;
- freely contributed Fellowship strength;
- the friendship proven through shared and contested evidence.

It is not a generic brighter lamp and not a superhero weapon. Its light is concentrated, grave and difficult to counterfeit because its power depends on freely contributed bonds.

The old light is preserved and rehoused through reforging. It is not treated as ordinary molten glass.

### Owner-approved forge structure

The current forge structure is intentional:

- Ruben, Max and Hendrik participate in the collective strike;
- Max delivers the depicted principal blow in `phial.three_smiths_strike`;
- Ruben performs the depicted fine assembly in `phial.ruben_assemble`;
- Hendrik appears in the approved white Kriegsfürsten armour;
- the Blaue-Glut anvil rune remains part of the visual grammar.

Do not revert this to a single-smith version without a new owner decision.

The forge may still unfold across two real days in the app. Existing clips can function as major beats inside that longer process.

---

## 8. Moriondo’s anger

### Performed rage

Performed rage is calculated intimidation:

- eyes continue tracking the target;
- balance and timing remain controlled;
- he stops by choice;
- the purpose is to redirect, frighten or test.

### Genuine rage break

The genuine break follows evidence that Steven’s spider defeated his command:

- he briefly fails to process the accusation;
- tracking collapses;
- posture and language fragment;
- he attacks the glass despite knowing force will fail;
- the aftermath does not erase the humiliation.

Random insults cannot farm this response. The trigger is evidence-backed.

---

## 9. Cycle I outcome

Cycle I ends with a real victory:

- the counter-command is proven and transmitted;
- the lower brood is reversed;
- the mounts are released and return;
- the ancient binding is broken by the Phial;
- Jan is revealed and freed;
- Moriondo remains imprisoned;
- the Fellowship establishes one genuine failure in Moriondo’s design.

Moriondo may learn from the riders, but the ending must not claim that every setback was secretly planned.

---

## 10. Continuation

The interrogation engine is designed for later cycles.

Cycle II may begin with the glass door standing open while Moriondo remains inside. It must preserve Cycle I’s victories and ask a new question rather than undoing the rescue.

Persistent memory may include only in-game behaviour: released clues, bargains, challenges, predictions and rage triggers. It must not infer or exploit sensitive real-world traits.

---

## 11. Media and implementation boundaries

`glasgefaengnis` may contain:

- animations;
- posters;
- semantic cues;
- review sequences;
- canon-safe demonstration dialogue;
- provenance and checksums.

It must not contain:

- the master solution graph;
- private participant assignments;
- undiscovered evidence;
- server-authoritative progression;
- production secrets that would let players reconstruct the final answer from the public bundle.

The playable media prototype may demonstrate tone, but JGA Fellowship OS remains the authoritative game.

---

The following are archived concepts only:

- Moriondo as an immobile power under the Ostberge;
- Moriondo as an evil Tom Bombadil mirror;
- Moriondo commanding or originating the Blue Wizards’ arc;
- Moriondo voluntarily imprisoning himself;
- the mounts remaining physically in the stalls;
- a song rewriting the rider–mount bond;
- the glass-prison interrogation being solvable through a six-question regex riddle.

They may remain in version history or an explicitly deprecated archive, never in active copy.
